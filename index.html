Harvest Rainwater, Build a Better Future

Water is one of the most precious resources on Earth, yet millions of people face water shortages every year. Rainwater harvesting is a simple, eco-friendly solution that helps us collect and store rainwater for future use instead of letting it go to waste. By collecting rainwater from rooftops and other suitable surfaces, we can reduce water wastage, recharge groundwater, support agriculture, and create a more sustainable environment. Every home, school, college, and community can contribute to water conservation by adopting rainwater harvesting practices.

Methods of Rainwater Harvesting

Rainwater harvesting can be done in different ways depending on the available space, rainfall, and water requirements. The main methods help collect, store, and conserve rainwater for future use.

1. Rooftop Rainwater Harvesting

Rooftop rainwater harvesting involves collecting rainwater from rooftops using gutters and pipes. The collected water passes through filters and is stored in tanks for later use. It is one of the simplest methods for homes, schools, and commercial buildings.

2. Surface Runoff Harvesting

Surface runoff harvesting collects rainwater that flows over the ground during rainfall. This water is directed into ponds, reservoirs, or storage structures. It is especially useful for agriculture, community water supplies, and managing rainwater in open areas.

3. Groundwater Recharge

Groundwater recharge allows rainwater to seep into the soil and replenish underground water reserves. Recharge pits, trenches, and suitable recharge wells can be used for this purpose. Proper filtration and site assessment are important to prevent groundwater contamination.

4. Rainwater Storage Tanks

In this method, rainwater is collected from rooftops or other suitable surfaces and stored in tanks for future use. The stored water can be used for gardening, cleaning, washing, and irrigation, depending on its quality and intended purpose.

5. Recharge Pits and Trenches

Recharge pits and trenches are structures designed to collect filtered rainwater and help it infiltrate into the ground. They are useful in suitable locations where soil conditions allow water to percolate effectively. Regular maintenance prevents blockage and improves performance.

6. Traditional Rainwater Harvesting

Traditional systems such as tankas, johads, kunds, and baoris have been used for generations, especially in Rajasthan. These structures collect or store rainwater and demonstrate how local communities have adapted to water scarcity and dry climatic conditions.

Rainwater Harvesting Construction Guide

Building a rainwater harvesting system is an effective way to conserve water and reduce water wastage. Follow this step-by-step guide to understand how to plan, construct, and maintain a basic rooftop rainwater harvesting system.

1. Materials Required

Collect the following materials before starting construction:

  • PVC pipes and connectors
  • Roof gutters and downpipes
  • Rainwater filter or filter mesh
  • First-flush diverter
  • Water storage tank with a lid
  • Gravel, coarse sand, and filter media if required
  • Pipe clamps, sealants, and suitable fittings
  • Overflow pipe and drainage arrangements

2. Step 1: Select the Catchment Area

Choose a suitable rooftop or other clean catchment surface. Measure its area and inspect it for dirt, damaged sections, and possible sources of contamination. The roof area and local rainfall help determine how much water can be collected.

3. Step 2: Install Gutters and Pipes

Install gutters along suitable roof edges to collect rainwater. Connect the gutters to downpipes that direct water towards the filter and storage tank. Secure all pipes properly and provide a suitable slope so water flows without stagnating.

4. Step 3: Install a First-Flush System

A first-flush diverter directs the initial rainfall away from the storage tank. This helps remove some dust, leaves, and contaminants accumulated on the roof before cleaner rainwater enters the collection system.

5. Step 4: Set Up the Filtration Unit

Install a suitable filter to remove leaves, debris, and suspended particles. Depending on the system design, filter media may include mesh, gravel, or sand. Filters must be accessible for regular cleaning and maintenance.

6. Step 5: Install the Storage Tank

Place a suitable water storage tank on a stable, level base designed to support its full weight when filled. Connect the filtered water inlet and provide a covered, mosquito-proof opening, a suitable overflow pipe, and an accessible cleaning arrangement.

7. Step 6: Create a Groundwater Recharge Structure

If groundwater recharge is planned instead of storage, a suitable recharge pit or trench may be constructed after assessing the soil, groundwater conditions, and local regulations. Filter incoming water and ensure the structure will not introduce contaminants into groundwater.

8. Step 7: Test the System

Before regular use, test the system during rainfall or with a controlled water flow. Check pipe joints, filters, tank connections, and overflow drainage for leaks or blockages. Correct any problems before operating the system routinely.

9. Maintenance and Safety

  • Clean rooftops and gutters before the rainy season.
  • Inspect and clean filters regularly.
  • Keep storage tanks covered and mosquito-proof.
  • Check pipes, joints, and overflow outlets for blockages.
  • Never enter a storage tank or pit without proper safety procedures.
  • Test and treat water appropriately before drinking.

Important Note

Construction requirements vary according to roof type, rainfall, soil conditions, groundwater depth, and intended water use. Consult a qualified professional for structural work, large storage tanks, and groundwater recharge designs.

Government Support for Rainwater Harvesting

Explore government schemes, agricultural subsidies, local authority guidance, and community initiatives that may support rainwater harvesting and water conservation across India, especially Rajasthan.

1. Jal Shakti Abhiyan: Catch the Rain

Promotes rooftop rainwater harvesting, groundwater recharge, restoration of water bodies, and public participation. It does not guarantee a cash subsidy for every household.

Official Website ↗

2. MGNREGA Water Conservation

Eligible rural public works may include ponds, water-harvesting structures, and watershed-related works. Projects require approval through the programme's local processes.

Official Website ↗

3. Pradhan Mantri Krishi Sinchayee Yojana

Supports eligible irrigation, watershed development, and water-management activities. Benefits depend on the scheme component and current state implementation.

Official Website ↗

4. Atal Bhujal Yojana (Atal Jal)

Focuses on community-led groundwater management and water security in selected areas. Check for a current extension or successor programme before applying.

Official Website ↗

5. AMRUT 2.0

Supports eligible urban water projects and water-body rejuvenation through urban local bodies. It is not generally a direct household tank subsidy.

Official Website ↗

6. Jal Sanchay Jan Bhagidari

Encourages community participation in water conservation and recharge structures. Assistance depends on local projects, funding arrangements, and implementation.

Official Website ↗

7. Mukhyamantri Jal Swavlamban Abhiyan 2.0

Rajasthan's water-conservation programme supports planned village-level water-harvesting, watershed, and water-body works. Contact your Gram Panchayat or district office.

Rajasthan Government ↗

8. Rajasthan Farm Pond Subsidy (Khet Talai)

Eligible farmers may receive assistance towards approved farm ponds. Published scheme information lists 60% of approved cost, up to ₹63,000 for an unlined pond or ₹90,000 for a plastic-lined pond, whichever is lower. Conditions apply.

Check Scheme ↗

9. Rajasthan Diggi Construction Assistance

Eligible farmers in qualifying canal-command areas may receive assistance for irrigation storage structures. Published information lists 75% of unit cost, up to ₹3 lakh, subject to current eligibility conditions.

Check Scheme ↗

10. Rajasthan Jal Hauz Assistance

Assistance may be available to eligible farmers for approved agricultural water-storage tanks. Published information lists 60% of cost, up to ₹90,000, subject to capacity and other requirements.

Check Scheme ↗

11. PM-KUSUM Solar Pump Support

Eligible farmers may receive assistance for solar pumps under applicable scheme components. This supports solar pumping equipment, not the construction of a rainwater tank.

Rajasthan PM-KUSUM ↗

12. Jaipur Development Authority Guidance

JDA provides rainwater-harvesting guidance, standard drawings, and indicative costs for certain roof-area ranges. Published cost figures should not be treated as a guaranteed subsidy.

JDA Information ↗

13. Local-Body Water and Sanitation Grants

Eligible local bodies may use permitted grants for water and sanitation priorities. Contact your municipality or Gram Panchayat to learn about approved local projects.

Panchayati Raj ↗

14. Jal Jeevan Mission

Village water-supply planning may include source sustainability, groundwater recharge, and water conservation where permitted under approved plans. It is not an automatic household grant.

Official Website ↗

15. Watershed Development Programmes

Eligible agricultural and community projects may include check dams, ponds, recharge structures, soil-moisture conservation, and watershed restoration.

Official Website ↗

16. Building Rules and Rainwater Harvesting

Some building categories and locations may require rainwater harvesting under applicable regulations. Requirements vary by property and local authority.

Rajasthan Urban Development ↗

17. CSR and Community Funding

Companies, non-profits, and community organisations may support local water-conservation projects through CSR funding or donations. Funding is project-specific and not guaranteed.

How to Apply for Government Support

  • Homeowners: Contact your municipality or development authority about applicable rules, approved designs, and any active subsidy.
  • Farmers: Visit the Raj Kisan Portal or contact your Agriculture Department or e-Mitra centre.
  • Villages and communities: Contact your Gram Panchayat, Block Development Office, or district watershed office.
  • Before construction: Verify eligibility, application dates, documents, and prior-approval requirements.
